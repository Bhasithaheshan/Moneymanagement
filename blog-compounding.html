How Compounding Works in Trading (And Why Small Targets Add Up)

Money Management · 6 min read

A 5% daily target sounds unremarkable. A 25% one sounds aggressive. But say those numbers out loud for a week straight, and the difference between "modest" and "aggressive" compounding becomes obvious fast — for better and for worse.

The Basic Math

Compounding simply means each day's gain (or loss) is calculated on top of the previous day's balance, not the original starting amount. If you start with $100 and target a 5% daily gain, hit five days in a row, your balance doesn't grow by $25 (5 × $5) — it grows to roughly $127.63, because each day's 5% is calculated on an already-larger number.

Stretch that same 5% daily target across 20 trading days (roughly a month of Monday-to-Friday trading) and $100 becomes over $265 — without ever touching your original capital. That's the appeal of compounding: small, consistent numbers stack into something that looks disproportionately large over time.

Where the Math Gets Dangerous

The same exponential curve that grows your account also grows your losses if targets are set too aggressively. A trader chasing 20–25% daily is implicitly betting on an unrealistic win rate sustained over dozens of consecutive sessions. One or two stop-loss days at that scale can erase a week of gains — and because percentages compound in both directions, recovering from a large drawdown requires an even larger percentage gain just to break even.

For example, a 50% loss on a given day requires a 100% gain the next day just to return to where you started. This asymmetry is the single biggest reason aggressive daily targets are harder to sustain than they look on a spreadsheet.

Why Realistic Targets Beat Exciting Ones

A lower, more repeatable daily target — something you can realistically hit on an average day, not just a great one — tends to produce smoother, more sustainable compounding. It's less exciting week to week, but it's far less likely to be wiped out by a single bad session.

This is also why weekly withdrawals matter alongside compounding: taking a portion of profit off the table regularly means a single bad stretch can't erase months of consistent, smaller wins.

The Takeaway

Compounding rewards consistency, not heroics. Before setting your daily target, ask honestly: "Could I realistically hit this on four out of five trading days, most weeks?" If the answer is no, the number is probably too aggressive — no matter how good it looks compounded across a spreadsheet.

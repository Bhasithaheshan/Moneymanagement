Position Sizing 101: The 1–2% Rule Explained

Risk Management · 5 min read

Position sizing answers a deceptively simple question: how much of your capital should be in play on any single trade? Get this wrong, and even a good strategy can ruin an account. Get it right, and even a mediocre strategy can survive long enough to be improved.

The Core Idea

A widely referenced starting point is to risk no more than 1–2% of total trading capital on any single trade or position. If you have $1,000 in your account, that means limiting a single trade's potential loss to roughly $10–$20 — not the entire trade's stake, but the amount you actually stand to lose if it goes against you.

The logic isn't magic — it's about survivability. At 2% risk per trade, it takes around 35 consecutive losses to wipe out an account. At 20% risk per trade, it takes about 5. Strategies and markets both have losing streaks; the question is whether your position sizing lets you survive one.

Why Smaller Is Usually Smarter Early On

New traders are often drawn to larger position sizes because the payoff feels more meaningful — a 2% risk on a small account can feel too slow. But the purpose of small, controlled sizing isn't to get rich quickly; it's to stay in the game long enough to find out whether your approach actually works, without one unlucky sequence ending the experiment prematurely.

Position Sizing and Daily Stop-Loss Work Together

Per-trade position sizing controls a single trade's downside. A daily stop-loss controls the day's total downside across multiple trades. Both matter — a trader can follow strict 2% per-trade sizing and still blow past a reasonable daily loss limit by taking many trades in a row after a string of losses. This is one reason a daily, account-level stop-loss rule remains valuable even alongside careful position sizing.

A Simple Way to Apply This

  • Decide your maximum acceptable loss per trade, as a percentage of total capital (many traders use 1–2%, adjusted for their own risk tolerance)
  • Recalculate your position size as your capital changes — a 2% risk on a $1,000 account is a different dollar amount than 2% on a $1,500 account
  • Keep this rule fixed regardless of recent wins or losses; resist the urge to size up after a win or "average down" after a loss

The Takeaway

Position sizing is one of the few trading decisions you can fully control before you ever place a trade. It won't make a bad strategy good, but it can be the difference between a losing streak that's survivable and one that ends your account.
